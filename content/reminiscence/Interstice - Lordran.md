# 01. Awakening.
The Northern Undead Asylum was peaceful, in its own way.

One could easily be lulled to sleep by the tread of whatever jailer had been placed over charge of the place. One of the many demons roaming the land—it thought it had seen it, once, but now that memory was all too faded.

Peace is, of course, dull. Imprisonment without end, for those who cannot end themselves, is even duller. One could not re-sharpen the edge without a millennia to work and the gods' greatest hammerwork.

...It had begun to think back to what remained of memory of the time before, of life outside this prison, and even that was proving dull.

Time chips away at your edge, your sanity. Your history. Your legend, your self.

And so it thought, what can one do with infinite time and infinite nothingness? Perhaps burrow out of this prison, but to where? There is naught but cliffs and a world full of hate outside.

*Would it be so bad to continue this solitude until the end of the world?*

---

A body lands in the cell.

It takes it a moment to understand that a change has occurred. Another to remember how to move, to adjust it eyes to focusing again.

Someone looks down at it, as it stirs and rises. They wave, and then scamper off across the roof without another word.

*Incomprehensible.*

Its skin and muscles are still taught and thin, but it takes little effort to move as one always has. It investigates this mysterious present.

Hollowed, just the same as itself. Barely anything to it—but 'barely' places this newcomer (or its remains) as one of the guards, not a prisoner. Its fingers quickly seek out a key from the body—one thing that never changes is where those keys tend to be kept, no matter *how* much time passes.

Its hand closes around the key, and, adjusting its armor, it rises.

*Very well*, it thinks. *Let's see what the point of all this is.*

---

It's sent flying through a wall by some inane trap of a sphere set up on one of the stairs. Though it does not vocalize, it curses all the same the architect of this place and its keepers. *Demons on the rooftops, boulders on the stairs. What's next, spikes!? And if your traps will break the cells in, what's the blazing point of—*

That figure from earlier sits slumped in the corner.

Looking up, there is clearly another hole of violence. The only thing that comes to mind is that the demon from earlier got them, somehow, before jumping down. It thanks the gods that it doesn't seem it can fly, but what was it up there for in the first place? This prison is so much older than it ought to be. There's not a single chance this will last until the end of the world.

"Oh, you..."

They'd (he'd?) noticed it, standing there.

"You're no Hollow, eh?"

Debatable, but certainly not as far gone as it had thought. The gormless dregs that litter the halls are another sort entirely.

The person awaits an answer, perhaps, so it...shrugs. It gestures to the roof with its sword.

"Ah, thank goodness. I'm done for, I'm afraid..."

*That's not much of an answer... Though, once can only ask so much from someone on death's door, in a way.*

"I'll die soon, then lose my sanity."

*That seems needlessly pessimistic. Is that why all these other fellows went so Hollow? No sense for doing anything with failure besides wallowing in it? Take up a hammer or plow and see if you still feel the same when your field goes under.*

"I wish to ask something of you."

*Bah. So be it.*

It tries to speak again, but failed. Perhaps from time, or the mental state, it is not able to turn these thoughts into sounds. Gestures still remain, but that would be the limit.

"You and I, we're both Undead... Hear me out, will you?"

It nods, and sheathes its weapon.

"...Regrettably, I have failed in my mission."

*...How so? Just pick yourself up off the ground and keep going. There's a bonfire just down the stairs, even.*

But with no way to express that and no wish to make him doubt his earlier decision, it just stands there, listening to him regale his history and prophecy, as a courtesy to the dying.

Perhaps old habits die harder than the living do.

As he puts it, he hails from Astora originally, with an old saying motivating him in his family. 'Thou who art Undead, art chosen'. It knows not where he would have originally escaped to the roof from, or even away to after his exit, but perhaps he had spent all he had contending with that large demon pacing the main room now.

Oscar hands it his Estus flask and a key to one of the other doors. None to the main one, so the jailer would still need to fall, but perhaps there is something that can be done with the balcony...

"...Now, I must bid farewell. I would hate to harm you after death... So, go, now. And thank you..."

It clasps its fist over its chest in a crude salute, and slowly steps to leave.

*A right confusing one, but...*

It pauses in the door.

*I can't say I hate the hero type.*

It offers him a wave, and starts moving.

*May we meet again, Oscar.*

---

As the sword sinks into the demon's head—*Dimwitted. Slow. Weakened. And still a threat to my life.*—it thinks about names, about weapons, about quests.

As the demon's great-hammer crashes into another of the room's pillars, into the floor, it thinks about what it is. *Who* it is, who it *was*, for the first time in a long time.

There is no answer, not in the usual sense.

The lingering Humanity in her quivers in response to the fight, in response to the taste of the soul and its dregs slathered across the walls. Something momentary, something *violent,* 

and then its sword finds a place firmly in the demon's throat, while the jailer struggled to wrench the tool from the wall again.

It still cannot speak, but it screams.

*A weak vengeance this is, but vengeance nonetheless. Depart from here, you demon.*

It is a paltry amount of soul that it captures from the demon, considering its size, but enough to galvanize the spirit to push open the massive doors leading toward the cliff face.

*Perhaps...that shall be my quest.*

The demons of the world, the foes of stature too great for a hapless hero, of resilience too deep for even the stout-hearted.

The Hollows and Undead will remain, until the end of time. But there are some creatures that *can* still be killed, and there is some sort of hero out there, for sure. 'The fate of the Undead' is too great to rest on the shoulders of one.

It looks up toward where Oscar likely still rests, and then steps out, feeling the world again, feeling the isolation anew as it approaches the precipice.

*Then, what shall my name be? What was it ever before?*

It feels the call of the hammer, the roar of the fire. Things that may have meant something to its past.

It stands on the precipice itself, a world away from the land of the decrepit gods.

It thinks of the coiled sword embedded in every bonfire. Perhaps it made one itself, or perhaps it did not, but it feels an attachment.

It formulates a name, but one that can never be spoken unless it reclaims its tongue.

*—!!*

A massive bird rushes up from the cliff face below, grasping it in its talons. Before it can react, it is taken, and whisked away somewhere. Somewhere far, far beyond this land, somewhere that is another one.

---

# 02. Lordran.
It is thrown down roughly on another cliffside, not even given the time in flight to understand where it was taken.

The only thing that calms its panic is the fire blazing, as tall as a body, from a bonfire in the center of a small... It seems to be a gathering place.

"Well, what do we have here? You must be a new arrival."

Its head turns toward the source of the thin voice, though it keeps the bonfire in periphery. A chain-clad man of dour appearance sits against one of the crumbling walls of this place, no more interested than a corpse with time to kill.

"Let me guess. Fate of the Undead, right? Well, you're not the first."

It stands with a start. *Not the first? Then, Oscar...* Did he not know, or did he try anyway? But no... No, of course he wasn't the first. Not after all this time. *Then, how many...?*

"But there's no salvation here. You'd have done better to rot in the Undead Asylum... But, too late now."

*It was a pleasant rot, I'll give you that.*

Its face settles into placidity or thought as it moves to sit at the bonfire. The man says nothing for a moment, and it sinks into the feeling of something so large, so warm, so—

There is a shift. The Humanity resurfacing within her finds that its body begins to fill out again, at some cost unclear. Perhaps the fragment of the demon that she had stolen with its life and soul, but...

*Curious.*

She feels her throat, now remade, as her vessel grows slightly, the loose soul slotting into place inside her. It seems that the pieces are all there, but still, there is no way to speak besides to grunt or groan. Has she just truly forgotten? She looks at the man again from under her helmet, and shrugs.

*Would that I could say one word at a time, even. Ask what any of this is about.*

The man sneers at her.

"Well, since you're here... Let me help you out. There are actually two Bells of Awakening. One's up above, in the Undead Church." He gestures up past the trees overhead. She cannot make out where, but curiously, she can hear the echoes of bells ringing anyway. "The other is far, far below, in the ruins at the base of Blighttown." This gesture is just in a vague direction outward. She stands and looks, but all there is is the ringed repetition of walls far below, defense against who-knows-what.

She turns back to him.

"Ring them both, and something happens... Brilliant, right? Not much to go on, but I have a feeling that won't stop you. So, off you go. It is why you came, isn't it? To this accursed land of the Undead? Hah hah hah hah..."

*That's... Well, I suppose it would not be incorrect, in a sense.*

She has no delusions that she is the one Chosen by whatever legend or prophecy—an ill-written one considering it seemed it could fit any Undead out there—but she finds herself at least interested in seeing what this business of Fate is about.

She steps down the stairs cautiously around the edge, looking for another view out on the land of Lordran, this one that she heard tell of for so long.

*It looks... Well this is just the blighted Asylum again, is it not!?*

Her grip tightens on the stones, thinking how ironic it is that this land seems as quiet and Hollowed as the place she had been residing in for so long.

*...Why does* this *bring me so much anger? I thought I had lost that capacity.*

She turns around, confused, ready to ponder it, and is stunned to see another woman, behind her.

In a cell, underneath the bonfire.

She stares and is stared back at. None exchange any words or gestures, just the gaze.

*Is she... No, what is this? Is this what it means to Keep the fire?*

The vestiges of some memory, some lore of the land she had used to know, poke out from the emptiness, but fail to coalesce. The term, and nothing more, is what she can recall.

She takes a step toward the Keeper, and then pauses.

*Would. That. I. Could. Speak.*

The frustration with herself mounts again, and she nods at the Keeper, and walks back up.

*If that is the way to Blighttown, then I shall go there once I can speak to her at all. There* must *be a way.*

---

Walking past the despondent fellow, she scouts the outer shell of this place of rest, and comes up with a few scraps of Humanity and a few incendiaries. A curious mix, but beyond that...

The question is a difficult one: *Up? Or in?* Up leads to the first of the Bells of Awakening mentioned, but with them ringing already, and the other way...oddly distasteful, she is left with only the option to go past the graveyard opposite this bonfire. There are other weapons left there that she can see from the grounds, as well as a large number of stray bones. It's enough to make anyone wonder what goes on in these parts, but she *has* heard tell of some necromancy. Perhaps someone has been pilfering...

---

# 03. A chasm of death.
*Flames and lightning take you allll!*

She is in far too deep.

It had started with a simple exploration. The skeletons were tougher than she expected, and what would that bode for the ones with flesh still on them? So she poked around the shrine to find some other gear, and started looking for who might be leaving all those messages around (she really wanted to see if she could borrow or buy off whatever they were using), and then in a blur of bones, bludgeoning, and blood, she suddenly had realized she was two bonfires down (if that's even a proper yardstick) in whatever catacombic construction lay beneath the...

Well, it was no church. No bell, no place to gather. Perhaps a shrine? Past tense? She would have to ask, if she was ever able to communicate again. The one man she passed at one of the large mechanisms was curious if she was a cleric, but all she could do was shake her head, and he seemed to lose interest.

*Sigh.*

A wordless breath escapes her, as she looks at her options.

One. Fight back to the shrine. Two. Keep going down.

Neither seem great, mostly because there's far too many skeletons around, and this wouldn't be the first time she's been taken down (fully or nearly) by a swarm of them. At least their usual habits seem to be fully static, baked into their bones and what soul still clings to them, but sprint all the way back topside is quite dicey because of the ones she'd already run past.

The necromancers seem to lack Undeath themselves, which is one consolation in this darkness (especially as she had to use all of those incendiaries to take one out across a gap). Another is that she is slowly adjusting more to her body. Spare soul is few and far between with the reanimation and the thin bones (*Seriously, how do those hit so hard!?*), but it gives her some comfort to release some of her bre..theren..? from some of their forced Undeath?

*Sistheren? Sib... Sibtheren. No.*

The words elude her. Perhaps it is just 'bretheren'. Perhaps she's forgotten her own tongue, in more ways than one.

She sits back against the walls of the cave for another hour, thinking over that, before something seems to call to her ears.

Something rhythmic, quiet. But unmistakably metal-on-metal.

She makes a fist and holds her weapon up, in the faint firelight. Compared to the shrine, this one is all-too-feeble, and barely fills her Estus. But it's enough to gleam by, and she knows that this sound is of someone smithing, or at least repairing.

It's a thought that has been sticking with her more, as she thinks on her name and her history.

She's quite sure she was a smith, at one point. Perhaps before the Curse, perhaps after. What did she make? And would she be able to pick that up again?

A smith needs fire, metal, and a place to work. The bonfire is decent enough for repairs, perhaps a small modification, but she has no tools, and she has no spare metal. The few weapons she's picked up (a morningstar, a winged spear, and a lucerne—*I suppose it makes sense I know these names*) seem valuable for handling the creatures that dot the caves and hills, but they too will need some repair eventually.

Perhaps she can find who that is down there, and by then, be strong enough to risk the run back up in the dark.

Perhaps the bones will find better rest, then.

---

"Be gone with you. You'll spoil my focus."

*...Well, it's a smith of a sort, at least.*

It was a laughable series of events, honestly. One of the necromancers had lurched backward seeing her emerge from the wrong direction (*Fair enough, I would have too. I thought I was heading back up to the bridge!*), and then a pair of skeletons missed their steps on the way down to challenge her. She looked down, and saw a *slightly* dangerous drop, but a passage open below near where they had fallen, and with a bit of luck she got herself down safely.

Which brings her to where this blacksmith is, and thankfully, out the other side of the wall.

She sits for a while at the bonfire, letting her Humanity sift back through her, after so much had left her frame.

Hollowing, in the physical sense, she is familiar with. Spiritually, she thinks she understands it, but for whatever reason, it never came for her either on route to nor during her stay at the northern Asylum.

*I wonder what does it, then. Why Oscar seemed to fall so quickly.*

The hammer strikes continue. She wonders, what was this one in life? Or was it born into bone, somehow? There are any number of oddities in the different lands, after all.

*But again, I cannot even ask.*

The hammer strikes pause.

"...Normally I'd say, 'your presence disturbs me', and push you back off into these Catacombs."

The smith turns to look at her, just with its head.

"But you seem familiar with this work, somehow."

She nods. Then, she gestures to her tongue and makes a cross over it, indicating she can't speak. It seems to understand.

"Bah. A shame, then. Neither warrior nor cleric, and yet somehow fallen through *my* roof."

It swivels back and brandishes some tools.

"For a fellow smith, I'd be willing to part with a few of these. But only for soul. Stuff's hard to find in these walls."

She makes the motion of a chuckle, and fishes out a few handfuls of soul to part with. All she's collected that hasn't entered her own. The other smith seems to find it plenty when she offers, and she is given a suite of tools with which to finally upkeep her own gear again.

*Ahh, how I have missed this. To think this even outlasts my memory.*

"Now then."

She wipes the smile from her face as best she can. Surely it means to tell her to leave it to focus, which she will soon.

"Undead, are you?"

*Oh, that's not where I thought this was going.* But she nods anyway.

"Bah. Come to the wrong place, my friend. The bells aren't here."

*Oh. Wait, really? Then what's the point of...*

"Ahh, there's still the bastard in his coffin, if you have any more bones to pick with the necromancers. But go no further. Find your way back above, unless you have reason to return. Plenty of smiths aboveground, unless they've all gone Hollow."

It spoke with a grave seriousness, the humor of which description was not lost on her. But she nods.

"Alright. Now! Off you go! I've prattled enough for months."

She tips helm and, after packing up at the bonfire, steps out into the catacombs once more.

# 04. The echo of the quest.
It slumps, staggers, back into the shrine. Barely still human in appearance, thanks to a rest on the way back up, but existentially exhausted and drained.

The skeletons turned out fine. Most of them. With some tuning and bolstering together with the smith down in the walls, the morningstar was crushing through what could not be avoided, and it had started working on the lucerne.

The black-clad knight, too. It seemed draped in titanite itself, in a way, but its senses were dulled in the dark just as the explorer's were, and it went down without much of a fight.

Even the *necromancer*, the grand orchestrant of the entire catacombs by the sounds of it, was of no concern. A passing paladin, or the specter of one, happened to enter at the same time, and it was dispatched without more than a minute of trouble. It sort of reflexively clutched at the enchanted bones in its pocket after picking up a rite it had left, seeing everything else in the area, and from there...

The problem was the *bone-wheels*. Or *whatever* the people here call those.

It picks out the last remaining bits of spikes, bone, stone and blade from its armor. Again. Who knows how many times this makes it.

No one seemed to comment on its delay in returning—not the looter, not the crestfallen, obviously not the fire-keeper. That was some comfort in itself.

More connected to the keeper jailed beneath the fire than anyone else it had known so far. Something rather tragic to that.

It plants itself face-first into the dirt next the bonfire, feeling the warmth, and sleeps.

---

"Thank you kindly. Nee hee hee hee hee!"

The laughter echoes behind it as it steps back toward the burg's bonfire (the only one, from the looks of it). It clutches a pair of orange soapstones, and soon pockets one in its belongings, properly catalogued. The other, it places in a pocket for easy access.

*Finally*.

Through luck and circumstance, the merchant lurking on the balconies of the burg had collected these from some hapless sorts before it, and after some gesturing and trial-and-error, was convinced to sell them over. It then enabled the purchase of some repairing powder, which both parties were happy about.

It picks the stone back out and draws a line in the air.

`Elation.`

The words are carved in the air, and linger for a time, before it erases them.

It does not really understand the mechanic of these stones or how their messages appear, but several of them have been helpful through the catacombs and up here, already. With a bolstered weapon and frame, the inhabitants of this burg are no trouble to push past or put down for a time, but that isn't to say that it isn't still slipping up.

*Oh well. Not a warrior, in the first place.*

It finds a place in the corner to take a rest.

---

"Ah, hello! You don't look Hollow, far from it! I am Solaire of Astora, an adherent of the Lord of Sunlight. Now that I am Undead, I have come to this great land, the birthplace of Lord Gwyn, to seek my very own sun!"

*Well if that isn't the most peculiar fellow yet.*

It happens upon a different sort, just past another of the large demons. With a properly kept weapon, and compared against the horrors of the catacombs, it felt almost... *easy*. Enough to make it wonder, was it really just a blacksmith, before? Is this what that life gives you? Or is there more at play, here?

It smiles at Solaire, of Astora. The name reminds it of Oscar.

"...Do you find that strange? Well, you should! No need to hide your reaction. I get that look all the time! Hah hah hah!"

*Ah, I see.*

`Charmer.` The words float out for a moment, before it erases them again.

"Oh, ah hah! So, I didn't scare you? I have a proposition, if you have a moment."

*Sure.* It nods.

"The way I see it, our fates appear to be intertwined. In a land brimming with Hollows, could that really be mere chance? So, what do you say? Why not help one another on this lonely journey?"

It tilts its head. *Intertwined?*

It nods, again.

"This pleases me greatly! Well then, take this. We are amidst strange beings, in a strange land. The flow of time itself is convoluted, with heroes centuries old phasing in and out. The very fabric wavers, and relations shift and obscure. There's no telling how much longer your world and mine will remain in contact."

*Ah. That would explain the paladin, then, and the other messages. There's no telling how long ago, or perhaps in the future, those were written.*

Solaire passes it a different kind of soapstone, in white, and continues.

"But, use this, to summon one another as spirits, cross the gaps between the worlds, and engage in jolly co-operation! Of course, we are not the only ones engaged in this. But I am a warrior of the sun! Spot my summon signature easily by its brilliant aura. If you miss it, you must be blind! Hah hah hah!"

It lets out a voiceless chuckle, and writes: `Saint? Happiness. Comfort.` The words that the orange soapstone can express are fairly limited, as if passing words through someone else's perception of the world to borrow their descriptions, but it feels that with practice, it can learn to wield them better. For example: `Bell, Intertwined?` Perhaps it is imprinting its own vocabulary over that of the old owner's.

"Hm? Ah, the ringing, is it? I'm not sure! Hah hah hah. Perhaps so, though." Solaire does not avert their eyes from the sun. "That is not my quest, but it is a fascinating one. I wish you luck."

`Comfort.` It waves Solaire farewell, and steps on to the bridge.

The thought scatters before it can find its end, thanks to an incoming blast of fire, but it *would* have thought: *What then does it mean, that so many Undead ring their own bell?*

Ringing the bells, at least, seems like it is a straightforward task. It will have to see what happens after that.

---

"Well, you must be a new arrival. I'm Andre, of Astora. If you require smithing... Well now, that's a well-kept weapon you have there."

`Appreciation.` It gestures to the tools. `Gorgeous view.`

Andre cracks a grin. "The nice thing about weapons... they never betray you. Plenty of reason to rely on them in this time."

It nods, rolling a shoulder to indicate past wounds. `Danger zone?`

"Ah, not here. This is the old church. It was abandoned in favour of the church that you passed through getting here, which is the worse one these days. There are paths leading from here to two forbidden planes: Sen's Fortress, and the Darkroot Garden. They attract all sorts of lunatics, no-one as cultured as yourself. It's fine to be Undead, but keep a level head, eh? Hah hah hah!"

`Gratitude.` It takes a moment to think, to put the words to the stones. `Fortress, Garden, forbidden?`

Andre sets down his hammer and rests his chin on his hands on it. "Sen's Fortress is an old proving grounds built by the ancients gods. It is the only route leading to the great Anor Londo. Of course, most fools can't even find their way into that fortified deathtrap. The Darkroot Garden I know little of, although I've heard rumours of a divine blacksmith who resides there. Those who get stumped in the Catacombs seek him for divine weapons."

It falls to its knees in despair, quickly writing out `Divine... Tears. Needed Divine.`

"Hah hah! Contended with the Catacombs, did you? Whatever for?"

`...Chance. Detour. Dashed through, too far. Amazing trap.`

"And yet you made it out... A fine piece of work, then."

`Blacksmith, appreciation.`

Andre's brows rise. "What, there's one lunatic down there, still? I thought those rumors were fake, myself. You're a lucky one."

It nods, and slowly rises. `Detour, Divine, Bell. Appreciation.`

Andre nods back, and returns to work on his items.

"'ll be seeing you, then. Be careful out there. Don't get yourself killed. Neither of us want to see you go Hollow."

It waves him off and returns to the bonfire.

As it sits there, letting the Humanity course back through her after finding a true point of rest in this aimless journey, she wonders.

*Is being killed what makes one Hollow, then?* It had happened to her several times by now, thanks to the absolute depravity of the bottom of the catacombs and a few different poor choices. Her Humanity certainly slipped away each time, but she had never truly *lost* it, or the capacity for it. *But why? It's not having a goal, or Oscar would not have been worried over it.* And she doesn't consider herself particularly resilient, especially next to a born warrior or hero.

The fire climbs, courtesy of the rite she had claimed.

*And is there a keeper within each of these, or just the one? Is this a good thing to do?*

The question cannot be spoken, and so remains unanswered.

# 05. Phantoms in the garden.
"...many of the giant trees here in Lordran are still descendants of those earliest archtrees. Though I could not say how many remain. But that is where yours comes from, most certainly."

The witch Beatrice finishes her explanation, and peers from under her hat at the wanderer sitting next to her. They had started discussing the stone golems that lay moss-covered in the garden and the magic they casted (which nearly had gotten her killed twice), and eventually the topic had gone toward gear.

"...Something bothers you."

`...Yes.` She bears in her hands a suit of armor that looks one-to-one with the one Oscar wore. The possibilities are as follows: This is not his, but one of a similar cloth, with a similar fate. This *is* his, and he has somehow already managed to die in a completely different place, without even the chance to go Hollow. This is his, and he is out there somewhere, gone Hollow without his armor.

She places the helm on the ground, and taps it once. `Saint.` *Savior, more like, but that one persists much longer than the rest.* `North. Mimicry? ...Despair?` She shakes her head, and sorts through her box to access her other shield. It is rare to use it this far from a bonfire, but there is no danger left in this portion of the garden.

"I see... I cannot offer you an answer, but I can at least walk with you for a time." She raises herself to her feet, and awaits the wanderer, shimmering in the pale bioluminescence of the plants. "...I said I could not say how many of the trees remain. But should you seek more of them, for any reason, I have heard rumor of something below even the swamps of Blighttown. Between this land of Lordran and the Abyss beneath it, there is something lingering of old." She begins walking up the stairs of the tower they stand in. "That is not my path, but perhaps there is some truth to it."

`Appreciated.` The wanderer follows her up. `...Path?` She gestures at the witch, to fill the words that still resist her wrist.

Beatrice shakes her head. "I couldn't say. Perhaps if I find it, ours may cross again. For now, let us see what remains of this smith you seek."

---

The Butterfly is almost not worth a sentence, but they both know that it would have proved near impossible if not for Beatrice's sorceries.

It is almost not worth more, if not for the question that it leaves.

*Who killed you, my man? Was it that creature?*

The wanderer leans down and gently wrests the Ember from the dead smith's grasp. Nothing remains to defend him, but it is entirely unclear what might have done this. Besides... 

She looks up at Beatrice, who shakes her head. "I am...not familiar with this creature. It's *possible* that it is what did this one in, but at the same time, how many years have passed in this place since he last did his work?" She hands over the lingering core of the butterfly, the coalescence of soul. "Perhaps one of the orthodox sorcerers might know."

She nods, and carefully lifts and stores the ember. *At least this, I can pass on to Andre.* She then bows to express her thanks to the witch. *I wonder how many other embers have been lost like this, across Lordran?*

Beatrice tips her hat. "What shall I call you, then? I have the sense that thinking of each other may help us encounter each other again, after we have gone our separate ways."

# E1. An epilogue, of sorts.
Hello again.

We were recently set upon by several lines of thought, in the wake of a few conversations about and depictions of *Dark Souls* series avatars.

All of us have always had issues with the concept of the "OC", but at the same time, issues with the concept of the "insert-avatar". This will be investigated as a tangent within the up-and-coming essay on existence and thought, but suffice to say that the *vibe* of "making an OC" has almost never worked for us and there is very little reason or way for any of us to ever end up in a game for several limitations.

So, then, where does that leave us?

Lynne Oscar Beatrice, Interloper of the Darkmoon and wandering smith, is an attempt instead to work the muscles of engaging with the world on a different level than simply placing someone external within it. It comes from translation, the want-need to obtain the entire context before making a later pass with greater understanding, but it also comes with a long time meandering in thought about what it means to make a character within these games, and what it means to engage with these games at all.

This is also touched on in the essay, but we take a lot of issue with wiki-brain and lore-first ways of thinking about series. Cults of information, cults of personality, cults of thought, Vaati will just make a video about it so why bother asking further questions. When was the last time that you, dear reader, sat down to ask what a game or piece of media might be saying, outside the context of what is already written on a wiki? # E2. An elaboration, of sorts.
I will make both an aside and further explanation on Gwyndolin and Anor Londo, because I think the Darkmoon plotline is one of the most interesting ones in this journey, and I also just want to write these thoughts down.

Gwyndolin, I am sure it is no surprise to hear, is a rather "controversial" figure within the wider online fandom. More specifically, how the fans treat her and her identity.

> [!quote] Moonlight Robe
> Robe of the Dark Sun Gwyndolin, protector of the forsaken city of Anor Londo.
> 
> The power of the moon was strong in Gwyndolin, and thus he was raised as a daughter. His magic garb is silk-thin, and hardly provides any physical defense.

This line is one that I've seen a lot of readings on, but one thing that stands out to me is that it's taken at face value that moonlight is equivalent to femininity within the pantheon of Lordran, and that is the entire reasoning.

Overall, while I agree that she/her are the true terms to refer to Gwyndolin with, I disagree with that default of framing. There are certainly clear echoes of the Yin and Yang sort of dichotomy with the Sun and Moon here on a surface-level thought through it, but there is a lot more going on here than the wikis would admit because the land of Lordran is more complicated than that.

I will first point to Seath, the Scaleless. The power of moonlight is a core part of him, and through his research, power, and the fragment of a Lord Soul he bears (also implied to be from Gwyn's as the one the Four Kings have is, from Gwyn's own soul's description), he is able to wield powers grandfathering sorcery, a curse and madness that is inherited by those that follow in his footsteps, and the ability to create life. This connection is furthered in DS3 with Gwyndolin wielding the Lifehunt Scythe as a miracle, Aldrich dreaming of Priscilla, and existing reasons to believe Priscilla is either a direct child of Seath or otherwise related to his work.

Next, to the dichotomy. There is Sun and Moon within faith, but Moon also has access to sorcery through faith (and there are ways to access miracle through knowledge, though rare). But to treat that as everything leaves out both Dark *and* Fire, as well as Death and Undeath. Sorcery and pyromancy additionally are said to share a source in faith before intelligence, and Dark can affect both of them, as well as miracles (not just showing up in the name of the Dark Sun and the Dark Moon, but implied to be related to Velka's miracles).

Additionally, what of Gwynevere? She is a princess of the sun, and at least in her capacity as an illusion and the "ruler" of Anor Londo in her father's absence, a successor. There is a clear example of the *nurturing* and *sustaining* aspects of the feminine archetype in Gwynevere as both a reflection of her father's sunlight and the miracles she shares to her followers. So what is it about the moonlight that caused Gwyn to raise Gwyndolin as a daughter?

Interestingly, it might be more to do with Gwynevere herself. The only other named child of Gwyn in the series is Filianore, and Gwynevere is recognized enough to take Gwyn's place (through the illusions, at least) as the ruler of the city of the gods. She is the sunlight to Gwyndolin's moonlight. It seems to me that the two of them and their abilities are paired, rather than being a relation to Gwyn's Cinder (more in play together with the Dark, as seen by the Undead Curse and the Darksign). Additionally, there is a sentiment I see around that daughterhood was *forced* upon Gwyndolin, but Gwyndolin herself is close to her sister Gwynevere enough to play as her, to Priscilla enough for Aldrich to dream of her through Gwyndolin, and to Velka in their endeavors to punish the guilty (Velka overseeing the sins, and the Darkmoon reprising for them). And then Velka, as another mirror of Gwyndolin in casting with the "wrong" tool, crossing boundaries, is another interesting relationship to look at, especially with her own relationship to the Painted World and Priscilla.

Also, like, nobody seems to bat an eye at Gwyndolin's snakes much? What's up with those. How is this compulsory femininity. Girls when they...when they what. What are they they-ing? They're also another aspect she shares with Priscilla, I think, though the apparent or real lack of a cohesive lower body is *unique* in the series, at least by my recollection. The only other snakes like that in the game are in Dark*root*, but I haven't been able to find a further connection to anything there, and beyond this game...? Nothing at all? Who was her mother?

Anyway, gender. There's actually a *lot* happening with gender within Anor Londo. Ornstein and Smough, two forms of masculine archetypes (the noble warrior and the cruel executioner) are illusions, with the only remaining one being the giant smith off to the side (steady, silent, forgotten caretaker and handyman). On both sides of the city are feminine archetypes, in the Fire Keeper (beacon for the wanderers and a point of rest) and the illusion of Gwynevere (warmth and reward), with the monstrous and despised (Priscilla) locked away much as Gwyndolin is, and also as the Brass Set describes the form of the Fire Keeper hidden under the brass that shares the color of the city (which is itself mirrored by Gwyndolin being the true face of sorts behind the illusion of her sister). Gwyn, the Great Man fallacy himself and a heroic male archetype, is symbolicly entombed together with the only one of his family who explicitly sided with the dragons as a final archetype (the traitorous son) in the same room where Gwyndolin resides. And on either side of the entrance is a path from and to constructed life (the Iron Golem as the soul reforged, and the Duke's Archives as the soul distorted).

Basically, what I'm saying is that Gwyndolin is a non-binary trans woman and an Empty Spaces girlsnakegodthing (this is why her counterpart in moonlight in *Elden Ring* is a plural dollgirl who is really into death and knifeplay and hangs out with wolves) who isn't out to the world at large but chills with her girl friends while they all struggle with identity and self-perception issues in a land that the gods have long abandoned, landlocked by the knowledge of how the world perceives artificial changes to life and anything either *strange* or related to their designated enemy of the dragons and draconic (especially since her family kills and locks those away) and still choosing to bear the responsibility of taking care of the other girls around her.

And that also makes Aldrich a chaser? I think?
